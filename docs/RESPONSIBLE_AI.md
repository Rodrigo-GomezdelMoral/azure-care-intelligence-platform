# Responsible AI impact assessment

Patient Risk & Adherence Intelligence's impact assessment follows the sections of the Responsible AI
Impact Assessment Template and the goals of the
[Microsoft Responsible AI Standard](https://www.microsoft.com/ai/principles-and-approach), together
with the responsible AI guidance of the
[Azure Well-Architected Framework](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/well-architected/) for
[AI workloads](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/well-architected/ai/responsible-ai).

> **All data is synthetic** (ADR-008): no number here is a clinical result. The model is described
> in [`model-card.md`](model-card.md), which this document links instead of repeating.

## 1. System information

| | |
|---|---|
| **System** | Patient Risk & Adherence Intelligence: the gradient boosting model `readmission-risk`, which estimates the probability of an unplanned readmission within 30 days and ranks a chronic-care cohort for preventive outreach under a fixed weekly capacity |
| **Lifecycle stage** | a demonstration on Azure Machine Learning: v1 is the champion the documents describe and v3 a challenger registered by the sweep ([`evaluation.md`](evaluation.md)); the online endpoint is deleted between demonstrations |
| **Features** | a training pipeline with a registration gate, a reviewed blue/green deployment, a weekly batch call list with the expected value of each call, SHAP drivers phrased by fixed templates, precedents by cohort similarity, a subgroup audit and a drift check; no language model and no agent (ADR-012, ADR-013) |
| **Geography and language** | one Azure region, Sweden Central ([`cost.md`](cost.md)); explanations in English only (ADR-012) |
| **Deployment mode** | an API behind Entra ID and the `Care.Read` app role, and online and batch endpoints with `aad_token` authentication ([`security.md`](security.md)) |

## 2. Intended uses

**Fitness for purpose.** The one intended use is prioritising preventive outreach: which two hundred
patients to call this week, and what it saves. That is a question of capacity, so the objective is
cost avoided per thousand contacts, not AUC (ADR-005): 875,000 € at the top 2 % against 423,000 €
at the F1-optimal threshold, assuming 35 € per contact, 4,800 € per readmission and 25 %
effectiveness, with a calibration error of 0.014 ([`evaluation.md`](evaluation.md)).

| Stakeholder | Benefit | Possible harm |
|---|---|---|
| Care managers and nurses, who read the list and call | a ranked list with the reason for each call | over-reliance on the ranking; a driver read as a cause |
| The medical director, who owns the programme | the expected value of the week's calls | a decision priced with assumptions that are not measurements |
| Patients, affected and never users | a preventive call when the risk is high | no call for a patient at risk; a score read as a judgement of the person |

**Goals of the Responsible AI Standard that apply:**

- *Impact assessment (A1):* this document, next to the model card it links.
- *Oversight of significant adverse impacts (A2):* section 3 treats the health-related score as a
  sensitive use and says who decides; the model card lists the out-of-scope uses.
- *Fit for purpose (A3):* evaluated on the question it answers, with calibration and three operating
  points on every run; the evidence is synthetic, so fitness for a real cohort is not shown.
- *Data governance and management (A4):* a cohort generated by documented code, registered with its
  row count, SHA-256, generator and seed; `prep` enforces the schema contract (ADR-008).
- *Human oversight and control (A5):* a nurse makes each call; a named reviewer approves every
  promotion (ADR-004); drift retrains and never promotes (ADR-007); the runbook has the rollback.
- *System intelligibility for decision making (T1):* every score carries its three largest SHAP
  contributions phrased by fixed templates, with the numbers next to the text (ADR-012).
- *Communication to stakeholders (T2):* the model card states intended use, out-of-scope uses and
  limitations; the README, the model card and [`evaluation.md`](evaluation.md) say that the data is synthetic.
- *Quality of service (F1):* AUC per subgroup, from 0.698 under 65 to 0.764 with public insurance.
- *Allocation of resources and opportunities (F2):* the list allocates outreach, so the audit
  measures selection, recall and precision per subgroup, and experiment 003 prices the mitigations.
- *Failures and remediations (RS2):* a failure before the promotion returns traffic to the colour
  that was serving; alerts on a failed run and on API server errors; rollbacks in the runbook.
- *Ongoing monitoring, feedback and evaluation (RS3):* `drift-check.yml` opens an issue and
  retrains when 30 % of the features have drifted (ADR-007); outcome monitoring is not implemented.
- *Disclosure of AI interaction (T3):* does not apply; nothing here converses or generates content.

## 3. Adverse impact

**Sensitive use.** A health-related score changes who receives attention, so this is treated as a
sensitive use even on synthetic data. Who decides:

- **The call:** a nurse. A score is a reason to call, never a reason to change treatment, deny care
  or classify a patient (model card).
- **The policy:** the programme owner, who decides whether to adopt a subgroup quota; fairness is
  reported, not gated (ADR-003).
- **The model:** the named reviewer of the `production` environment, whom the runbook asks to read
  the comparison and the subgroup audit of both versions before approving (ADR-004).

What can go wrong:

- **Limitations:** the model has learned the generator, not medicine, and its disparities are
  invented in size (ADR-008); with 120 contacts in the test split, one readmitted patient found or
  missed moves the headline by 10,000 € (ADR-003); explanations describe the model, not causes.
- **Predictable failures:** a patient at risk is left off the list (recall 0.038 without insurance
  against 0.086 with public insurance); a call reaches a patient who would not have been readmitted
  (precision 0.758 among the contacted); the cohort drifts; a worse model is promoted (section 5).
- **Misuse:** the score becomes a label in a patient's record, a criterion for coverage or a
  judgement of adherence, or is used on real patients without validation: the model card rules both
  out. A reviewer approves without reading the comparison the runbook asks for: nothing enforces it.

## 4. Data requirements

- **Training and evaluation data:** 40,000 synthetic patients and 57 features with OMOP CDM names:
  a seed population from published distributions, CTGAN on the seven root variables, and every
  dependent variable and the label recomputed by documented equations (ADR-008).
- **Personal data:** none. No real patient record was used, the only identifier is a generated
  `person_id`, and no tabular file has been committed ([`security.md`](security.md)). Real data would need an
  extract pipeline under a data processing agreement and a private network (ADR-014).
- **Sensitive attributes:** sex, age, language barrier, deprivation and insurance are in the cohort
  and audited; the socioeconomic features are kept because they identify who needs help most.
- **Lineage:** every registered version carries its metrics, data asset version and pipeline run as
  tags (ADR-003); `patient-cohort:1` cannot be regenerated byte for byte (ADR-008).

## 5. Summary of impact

| Harm | Mitigation | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A score used for a clinical or coverage decision | intended use limited to prioritising outreach; out-of-scope uses stated; the list behind the `Care.Read` role | [`model-card.md`](model-card.md), [`security.md`](security.md), ADR-014 |
| Unequal outreach across groups | subgroup audit on every evaluation, published with the metrics; the mitigation priced; the quota left to the programme owner | [`evaluation.md`](evaluation.md), `evaluation-v1.json`, experiment 003, `tests/test_evaluation.py` |
| A worse model reaches care managers | registration gate on the business metric, where ties lose; reviewed blue/green with a smoke check and rollback | ADR-003, ADR-004, `tests/test_evaluation.py`, `deploy-prod.yml` |
| A drifted cohort degrades the list unnoticed | schema contract in `prep`; drift check that opens an issue and retrains, never promotes | ADR-007, `tests/test_monitoring.py`, [`operations/runbook.md`](operations/runbook.md) |
| An explanation read as a cause, or invented | deterministic templates over SHAP, no language model; the model card says what they describe | ADR-012, `tests/test_serving.py` |
| Synthetic figures taken for clinical results | the synthetic-data note in the README, the model card and the evaluation; the assumptions stated with every euro figure | [`README.md`](../README.md), [`model-card.md`](model-card.md), [`evaluation.md`](evaluation.md), ADR-008 |
| Exposure of patient data | synthetic data only; Entra tokens at every hop and no keys | [`security.md`](security.md), ADR-008, ADR-014 |
| Realised outcomes not yet checked | outcome monitoring is designed and awaits an outcome feed | ADR-007 |
