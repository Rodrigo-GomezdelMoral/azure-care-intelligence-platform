# Model card · `readmission-risk`

**Version described:** 1 (champion) · **Trained:** 2026-10-20, pipeline run `red_collar_g75j7w4dyp` ·
**Data:** `patient-cohort:1`, **entirely synthetic** (ADR-008) · **Family:** gradient boosting
(`HistGradientBoostingClassifier` behind a `ColumnTransformer`, ADR-001) · **Owner:** the
repository author, as a portfolio demonstration

> **No number on this page is a clinical result.** Every patient in the training and test
> data was generated by `src/data/synthesize.py`. The figures demonstrate the lifecycle and the
> arguments; they say nothing about any real population.

## Intended use

The model estimates, for each patient in a chronic-care cohort, the probability of an unplanned
hospital readmission within 30 days, from adherence, prescription, contact, comorbidity,
measurement and socioeconomic features available in a care-management system. Its only
intended use is **prioritising preventive outreach**: ranking the cohort so that a care
programme with a fixed weekly capacity contacts the patients most likely to benefit, with the
expected value of each contact shown next to the score (ADR-005). The intended users are care
managers and the medical director who owns the programme.

**The system prioritises outreach. It never makes, recommends or replaces a clinical
decision.** A score is a reason for a nurse to call, not a reason to change treatment, deny
care or classify a patient.

## Out-of-scope uses

- Any decision that changes a patient's treatment, coverage, cost or access to care.
- Any use of the score as a measure of a patient's compliance, responsibility or merit; the
  adherence features describe circumstances (polypharmacy, copays, living alone, a language
  barrier), not character.
- Scoring populations unlike the training cohort (paediatric, acute, surgical) or feeding the
  score into another model without re-evaluation.
- Use on real patients without the validation this card cannot provide: the data is synthetic.

## Training data and provenance

40,000 synthetic patients, 57 features with OMOP CDM names, generated by a documented causal
structure: a seed population drawn from published adherence and readmission distributions,
CTGAN fitted only on the seven root variables under the documented categorical marginals, and
every dependent variable and the label recomputed by the same equations (ADR-008). The data
asset is registered with its row count, SHA-256, generator and seed. Readmission rate 0.189,
mean proportion of days covered 0.675. Splits are stratified 70/15/15 (seed 7); the test split
(6,000 patients, 1,133 readmitted) was touched once, by `evaluate`.

## Evaluation (test split, programme parameters of `.env.example`)

| Metric | Value | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| AUC | 0.756 | does the model rank readmitted patients above the others |
| Average precision | 0.459 | how concentrated the readmissions are at the top of the list |
| Brier score | 0.129 | are the probabilities honest |
| Expected calibration error (10 bins) | 0.014 | the same, by decile: predicted 0.045 to 0.558 against observed 0.052 to 0.565 |
| Precision among the 120 contacted (top 2 %) | 0.758 | of the patients called, how many would have been readmitted |
| **Cost avoided per thousand contacts** | **875,000 €** | the headline: net euros per thousand calls, at 35 € a call, 4,800 € a readmission, 25 % effectiveness |

Experiment 001: a logistic regression matches these numbers on this cohort (AUC 0.757,
865,000 € per thousand) with a model 37 times smaller, because the generator's label is
log-linear in the features; the boosted model is kept for the lifecycle it demonstrates, and a
real deployment should rerun that experiment on its own data. Experiment 002: the F1-optimal
threshold would ask for 1,863 calls on the same split at 423,000 € per thousand; the capacity
policy is the operating point.

## Fairness analysis

The audit (`src/fairness.py`) looks at the contacted set, not at the probabilities: for each
subgroup, the share contacted (selection rate), the share of its readmitted patients contacted
(recall) and the precision among its contacts, with the ratio of the lowest to the highest
group value as the disparity (1.0 is parity).

| Attribute | Groups (patients) | Selection rate | Recall | Precision | Worst ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sex | female 3,106 / male 2,894 | 0.020 / 0.020 | 0.086 / 0.074 | 0.836 / 0.678 | precision 0.81 |
| Language barrier | no 5,534 / yes 466 | 0.020 / 0.021 | 0.080 / 0.082 | 0.755 / 0.800 | selection 0.93 |
| Age band | under 65 2,256 / 65 to 74 2,170 / 75 and over 1,574 | 0.001 / 0.008 / 0.064 | 0.004 / 0.041 / 0.148 | 0.500 / 0.778 / 0.760 | **selection 0.014** |
| Deprivation | low 1,679 / medium 3,079 / high 1,242 | 0.015 / 0.018 / 0.031 | 0.075 / 0.068 / 0.110 | 0.808 / 0.709 / 0.795 | selection 0.49 |
| Insurance | public 3,560 / private 1,848 / none 592 | 0.021 / 0.020 / 0.012 | 0.086 / 0.082 / 0.038 | 0.763 / 0.784 / 0.571 | recall 0.44 |

What the table says: at a capacity of 2 %, the list is almost entirely patients aged 75 and
over (readmission rate 0.33 against 0.12 under 65), and patients without insurance are reached
less often than their readmission rate would warrant (recall 0.038 against 0.086). Both are
direct consequences of the generator's equations (age and deprivation raise risk; no insurance
is a proxy of deprivation); they are realistic in direction and invented in size. Experiment 003
(phase 9) measures what a mitigation costs in overall cost avoided. Fairness is reported, not
gated (ADR-003); gating on it is the programme owner's policy decision.

## Limitations

- **Synthetic data.** The model has learned the generator, not medicine. Nothing here
  validates the features, the base rate, the effect sizes or the disparities of a real cohort.
- The business metric is computed with programme parameters that a real programme must
  replace with its own, and with an effectiveness of 25 % that is an assumption about the
  intervention, not a measurement.
- The test split holds 120 contacts at the capacity cut, so the headline metric moves by
  10,000 € per readmitted patient found or missed; small differences between models are noise
  (ADR-003 discusses the margin).
- The model is static: it does not use the sequence of events, and features are a snapshot.
  Drift monitoring (phase 8) is the only guard against the cohort changing under it.
- Explanations (phase 7) are SHAP contributions translated by fixed templates; they describe the
  model, not causes.

## Ethical considerations

A risk score acted on by people changes who receives attention. The programme must contact
patients because they are likely to be readmitted, never withhold care because they are not;
the score must not become a label in a patient's record, a criterion for coverage, or a
judgement of adherence as behaviour. The subgroup audit runs on every evaluation and is
published with the metrics so that a disparity is a known fact before a model is promoted, and
promotion itself is a human decision with a named reviewer (ADR-004). The features that carry
socioeconomic information are kept because they are the ones that identify patients who need
help most, and the audit exists to make sure that is what they do.
